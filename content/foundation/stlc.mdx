## What this lesson covers

The **software testing life cycle** is the tester's operating system. SDLC decides how software is built. STLC decides how confidence is earned, recorded, and handed off.

- Six STLC phases and the artifacts each one produces
- Entry criteria, exit criteria, and suspension
- A worked checkout example you can copy into a real project

## Outcome

After this lesson you will be able to run a test cycle with a plan, a design, evidence, and a closure note — not just a pile of tickets.

<Info title="STLC is not a waterfall tax">
In Scrum you still plan, design, execute, and close. You just do it in hours or days per story, and you keep a thin suite living across sprints. The phases are activities, not calendar months.
</Info>

## The six phases

| Phase | Primary question | Typical artifacts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requirement analysis | What must we prove, and what is ambiguous? | Testable requirements, questions, RTM start |
| Test planning | How much, by whom, with which risks? | Test plan, estimates, entry/exit criteria |
| Test design (case development) | Which samples give the cheapest signal? | Cases, charters, data, oracles |
| Test environment | Can we even run the tests? | Env checklist, test data, access, stubs |
| Test execution | What happened, and is it a defect? | Results, bugs, retests, progress |
| Test cycle closure | What do we know, and what is leftover? | Summary, open risks, lessons learned |

Traceability (often a **requirements traceability matrix**) is the thread: every in-scope requirement maps to at least one test, every test maps to a result, every failure maps to a defect or a waived risk.

## Entry, exit, and suspension

**Entry criteria** are the gates before you spend execution budget. Examples:

- Stories have acceptance examples
- A build is installed in the test environment
- Payment sandbox credentials work
- Known blockers from last cycle are closed or explicitly waived

**Exit criteria** are the gates before you call the cycle done. Examples:

- All P0 cases executed
- No open Sev-1 defects
- Regression pack green on the agreed browsers
- Residual risks written down, not forgotten

**Suspension criteria** pause execution when continuing would waste time: environment down, blocking defect, or data refresh failed.

<Warning title="Exit criteria without numbers become vibes">
"Testing is complete when we feel good" cannot survive a release meeting. Prefer countable statements, plus an explicit waiver list for anything you skipped.
</Warning>

## Practical example: SAVE20 checkout cycle

Imagine Quality Central's sandbox cart. Scope for this cycle: apply a percent discount once per order.

### 1. Requirement analysis

- In: one coupon, one apply, totals on desktop and a 375px viewport
- Out: stacking two different coupons (later story)
- Ambiguity to raise: does a second click replace the first discount or add another 20% of the original subtotal?

### 2. Test planning (excerpt)

```text
Objective: Prove SAVE20 applies once; catch stacking and mobile overlap.
Risks:   Discount math, payment field occlusion under 768px.
Entry:   Build deployed; coupon SAVE20 enabled; test card available.
Exit:    P0 cases pass; no Sev-1 open; residual risks listed.
Suspend: Checkout returns 5xx for 15 minutes or coupon service is down.
```

### 3. Test design

Black-box samples:

| ID | Idea | Expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| QC-D1 | Apply SAVE20 once | 20% off subtotal, tax on discounted amount |
| QC-D2 | Apply SAVE20 twice | Still a single 20% — no stack |
| QC-D3 | Clear coupon | Original subtotal restored |
| QC-D4 | Viewport 375px | CVC and Submit both usable |

Exploratory charter (90 minutes): "Attack discount + validation. Note any way to submit without a valid email."

### 4. Environment

- Seeded cart with a known subtotal
- Coupon service flag on
- Do not use production PAN

### 5. Execution notes

You run QC-D2 and the total drops again. That is a **failure**. You file a defect with actual vs expected, then keep executing unblocked cases (QC-D4) instead of waiting. Confirmation testing happens after the fix; regression is the rest of the pack.

### 6. Closure

A useful closure paragraph:

> Executed 4 of 4 P0 cases. QC-D2 failed (discount stacking) — open Sev-2. QC-D4 passed on 375px. Gift cards still out of scope. Recommend blocking release of the coupon flag until QC-D2 is retested.

That paragraph is what stakeholders actually need. Screenshots live in the defect, not in the summary.

## Planning vs control

ISTQB splits **planning** (decide) from **control** (steer). During execution you compare progress against the plan: are P0s finishing? Did a blocker eat the budget? Control actions include adding a charter, dropping a P3, or calling suspension.

```text
Planned P0: 12
Executed:   9
Blocked:    2 (payment sandbox)
Deferred:   1 (gift card — out of scope after analysis)
```

Numbers like these belong in standup. They are STLC monitoring, not bureaucracy.
